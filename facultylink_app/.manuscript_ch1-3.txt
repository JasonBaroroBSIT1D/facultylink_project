CHAPTER I
INTRODUCTION
Background of the Study
State Universities and Colleges (SUCs) depend on their faculty members to deliver educational programs through their teaching work while they perform research and provide extension services to the community. The DBM–CHED Joint Circular (Department of Budget and Management [DBM] & Commission on Higher Education [CHED], 2022) provides the evaluation process used. The research demonstrates faculty members undergo evaluation to receive promotion and rank advancement and reclassification recognition for their professional development and academic accomplishments. Research shows that transparent promotion systems which award merit to faculty members their motivation levels increase and their productivity rises and their retention rates improve, which results in better institutional performance and student learning outcomes (Sharma & Jyoti, 2022; Nguyen et al., 2023).
The Department of Budget and Management (DBM) and the Commission on Higher Education (CHED) of the Philippines have published a Joint Circular which establishes the procedures for faculty members to achieve promotion and reclassification. Faculty performance assessment according to this circular uses Key Result Areas (KRAs) which include Instruction Research and Innovation Extension or Community Services and Professional Development. Each KRA is assinged a specific number of points, which are determined by documented achievements and supporting materials, including teaching records, research publications, and relevant certifications. Consequently, this evidence-based approach ensures that promotion determinations are based on quantifiable performance metrics and uniform standards (DBM & CHED, 2022).
Despite having a structured and standardized evaluation system in place, the preparation for faculty reclassification is a complex and demanding process. Many faculty members still use manual methods, spreadsheets, or general systems to organize their documents and calculate their accumulated points. Because official scoring happens during formal evaluations by institutional committees, faculty members often have limited ways to assess their readiness before submitting their materials.
As a result, several difficulties emerge, encompassing the flawed calculation of Key Result Area (KRA) points, the submission of incomplete or non-adherent documentary prerequisites, and ambiguity in assessing promotion eligibility. Manual processes can also cause inefficiencies, delays, inconsistencies, and human errors within evaluation procedures. Research suggests that dependence on manual administrative systems can detrimentally impact accuracy and decision-making, especially in intricate and rule-based evaluations (Aljawarneh, 2022). Moreover, the absence of specialized digital instruments constrains transparency and impedes faculty members' capacity to effectively monitor their performance (Bond et al., 2022; Zawacki-Richter et al., 2023).
This study introduces FacultyLink: An Intelligent Document-Validated Faculty Self-Assessment System for DBM-CHED Joint Circular Rank Upgrade and Reclassification. The system's primary function is to aid faculty members through automated scoring, document validation, and rank simulation, all of which are grounded in the Joint Circular's stipulations. By integrating technologies including Optical Character Recognition (OCR), rule-based validation protocols, and performance monitoring dashboards, the system endeavors to enhance document processing speed, reduce manual computation error in KRA scoring and provide clear monitoring of faculty compliance. Consequently, FacultyLink’s ultimate objective is to assist faculty in evaluating their preparedness, ensuring adherence to regulations, and streamlining the promotion and reclassification procedures. The system is considered intelligent because it applies rule-based decision logic aligned with DBM–CHED Joint Circular guidelines in evaluating and computing faculty performance.
Statement of the Problem
The process of applying for a faculty rank upgrade or reclassification, as outlined in the Joint Circular, often takes a long time and is quite complex. Faculty members must manually prepare, enter, and check many documents. This is done without a reliable system to accurately assess their qualifications before they submit their applications. This study specifically aims to address the following issues:
Lack of a dedicated self-assessment platform – Faculty members do not have access to a dedicated platform aligned with the DBM–CHED Joint Circular that enables them to evaluate their readiness prior to document submission.
Limitations in the computation on KRA points contribution allocation across different KRAs – Manual and spreadsheet-based systems often produce inaccurate computation of faculty points, including the distribution of contribution points in collaborative outputs and activities, resulting in delays, inconsistencies, and possible violations of DBM–CHED Joint Circular requirements.
Difficulty in monitoring compliance requirements - Faculty members face difficulties when they try to observe their advisership activities and research outputs and various necessary documents which results in incomplete or disorganized submissions. 
Risk of submitting incomplete or non-compliant documentary evidence - The missing validation mechanisms result in higher chances of document loss or insufficient documentation which results in point disqualification during the evaluation process.  
Addressing these problems will create more efficient processes while improving accuracy and transparency in the faculty rank upgrade and reclassification system which will enhance faculty morale and provide equal career development opportunities while the organization maintains compliance with Joint Circular requirements.
Statement of the Objectives
The main objective of the study is to design, develop, and evaluate FacultyLink, an intelligent document-validated faculty self- assessment system which includes rank upgrade simulation and improves KRA point accuracy while meeting Joint Circular requirements and providing clear faculty reclassification procedures for State Universities and Colleges (SUCs).
The following goals are thought to be carried out to reach this goal:
To design and develop a web-based and mobile application platform that enables users to upload, manage, and validate faculty documentary evidence in accordance with the DBM–CHED Joint Circular guidelines, including reviewer validation and feedback mechanisms prior to the official evaluation process.
To develop an automated scoring and assessment module that computes Key Result Area (KRA) points and automatically applies contribution allocation and scoring rules across different KRAs based on DBM–CHED Joint Circular guidelines, while providing a real-time self-assessment dashboard and rank upgrade simulation to identify missing competencies across all KRAs.
To evaluate the system in terms of functionality, accuracy and usability based on predefined evaluation metrics and user feedback.
Significance of the Study
This study provides important benefits to stakeholders who work at State Universities and Colleges (SUCs) because it solves problems which occur during faculty promotion and reclassification processes that follow DBM-CHED Joint Circular. FACULTYLINK system provides automated scoring and document validation and real-time monitoring and rank upgrade simulation module which lets users determine their eligibility and find their assessment gaps before they take the official evaluation.
The development of FacultyLink will benefit several stakeholders:
Faculty Members – The system enables faculty members to assess their promotion readiness through its automated scoring system which evaluates documents and provides rank upgrade predictions. 
Institutional Evaluation Committees (IEC) – The system enhances evaluation efficiency by organizing document submissions while checking their completeness and compliance before the evaluation process starts.
State Universities and Colleges (SUCs) – The system improves faculty evaluation processes through standardized assessment methods which operate under system-based assessment procedures. 
Administrators – The system provides administrators with structured reports and analytics which they can use to assess faculty readiness and track organizational performance patterns.
Future Researchers – The system provides a reference point for research studies that investigate intelligent faculty evaluation systems and decision support systems and simulation-based assessment models.
Scope and Limitation of the Study
The study investigates FacultyLink development which serves as a self-assessment system for faculty members who need to prepare their rank upgrade and reclassification process according to Joint Circular regulations. The system operates as an online tool which enables users to handle documentary materials while tracking their progress in achieving Key Result Areas (KRA) throughout their work. FacultyLink will be implemented as a web and mobile application that allows registered faculty users to log in, and upload documentary evidence related to their academic achievements which includes research publications and advisership records and supporting certifications. The system enables users to monitor their KRA score breakdown and review document completeness and simulate their potential eligibility for rank upgrade.
The system will be used by faculty members and designated evaluators within State Universities and Colleges (SUCs) as a preparatory and support tool for faculty evaluation.
However, this study has several limitations. The system does not replace the official evaluation conducted by authorized Joint Circular committees, as it is intended only as a preparatory and self-assessment tool. It is limited to assisting faculty members in evaluating their readiness for rank upgrade and reclassification rather than serving as a final decision-making authority. Furthermore, the system operates strictly within the scope of the DBM–CHED Joint Circular guidelines and may require updates if there are changes or revisions to the policies. The system requires an internet connection and cannot be accessed offline. The system does not verify the authenticity of uploaded documents and relies on reviewer validation.

Review of Literature 
Faculty Evaluation Systems and DBM–CHED Joint Circular
Faculty evaluation systems are necessary to create assessment methods which maintain fairness and transparency throughout the evaluation process of academic performance metrics. The Department of Budget and Management together with the Commission on Higher Education established DBM–CHED Joint Circular No. 3, s. 2022 (NBC 461 – 9th Cycle), as the governing document for faculty rank upgrade and reclassification processes in the Philippines. The framework assesses faculty performance through Key Result Areas which include Instruction Research and Innovation Extension and Professional Development (DBM & CHED, 2022). 
The multidimensional nature of KRAs introduces complexity in evaluation, particularly in weighted scoring systems and the assessment of co-authored research outputs. Manual computation processes are therefore prone to inaccuracies due to the need for precise calculations. Studies have shown that performance management systems in higher education play a critical role in improving faculty motivation, accountability, and overall work quality, while also supporting systematic evaluation and decision-making processes (Yawson & Paros, 2023).
Recent studies on higher education systems emphasize the importance of structured and quality-assured instructional leadership frameworks in improving faculty performance and institutional effectiveness. According to Aquino (2025), high-performing higher education institutions implement standardized evaluation frameworks which maintain consistent evaluation procedures while providing transparent assessment methods and supporting their institutional objectives. The frameworks enable universities to assess their faculty members through systematic evaluation methods which enhance their assessment results according to the DBM–CHED Joint Circular requirements that this research follows.
 Despite these advancements, existing evaluation systems still rely heavily on manual processes and fragmented tools, which lead to inconsistencies, computational errors, and limited transparency. The evaluation process needs a system like FacultyLink because it matches DBM–CHED Joint Circular requirements while delivering better evaluation results through accurate and efficient evaluation processes.
Digital Transformation in Academic Administration
Digital transformation has brought about major enhancements to administrative operations throughout higher education institutions. Mergel et al. (2021) showed that digital governance systems bring organizations three benefits which include increased operational efficiency and better transparency and improved service delivery. The research by Strohmeier (2022) demonstrated that digital human resource systems help organizations decrease their administrative workload while achieving better data integrity.
The evaluation systems used in higher education institutions achieve their intended purpose through quality assurance systems which assess their evaluation performance. Prado (2025) demonstrates that institutional quality assurance practiceslead to improved evaluation accuracy and evaluation consistency and evaluation accountability in academic programs. The study shows that automatic validation and compliance checking systems should be integrated through established validation and monitoring procedures because these systems require systematic validation and monitoring processes.
Academic institutions use digital systems to manage faculty information, assess academic outcomes, and support institutional decision-making processes. The systems offer basic record-keeping functions, but they do not include the required advanced assessment tools needed to evaluate faculty performance and make promotion decisions. The public sector continues to face digital integration difficulties because administrative systems need complete automation to manage complex evaluation work (Mergel, Edelmann, & Haug, 2021).
Many digital systems used in higher education institutions operate solely for administrative purposes, which include record-keeping and reporting, because they do not have advanced evaluation and decision-making functions.. This gap demonstrates the need for more advanced systems that integrate automation, validation, and analytics, which are addressed by the proposed FacultyLink system.
Optical Character Recognition (OCR) for Document Validation
The document processing procedure becomes more efficient because Optical Character Recognition (OCR) technology enables automatic text extraction from scanned documents, reducing the need for manual data entry. OCR systems, such as Tesseract, allow large volumes of document images to be converted into machine-readable text, thereby improving accessibility and processing efficiency (Hegghammer, 2022).
According to recent studies, the accuracy and effectiveness of OCR systems depend on the quality of input documents, including factors such as noise, blur, and document degradation (Alaei et al., 2023). Poor-quality images can significantly reduce text recognition performance, whereas high-quality documents enable more accurate extraction of information.
Furthermore, OCR-based systems improve document processing workflows through their ability to analyze extensive document collections at high speeds while delivering satisfactory accuracy rates (Hegghammer, 2022). Educational institutions use automated text recognition technologies to improve administrative operations through their ability to decrease manual tasks and enhance data management.
Despite the effectiveness of OCR technologies their performance hinges on the quality of documents and they do not provide systems for validating data accuracy and compliance. The FacultyLink system demonstrates the need for OCR technology to function with rule-based validation systems.
Rule-Based Validation Systems
Rule-based validation systems uses predefined logical rules which verify that data meets established standards. These systems provide transparency which helps stakeholders understand the complete decision-making process including its validation methods. The essential role of transparency together with explainability in decision-making systems receives emphasis from both explainable artificial intelligence and knowledge-based systems which need traceable and accountable operations (Borrego-Díaz & Galán-Páez, 2022).
Rule-based systems enable administrators to establish and control validation rules which create a standardized process for assessing data. The system provides users with better understanding of its results because they can understand how the system operates through its complete transparency which serves as a major benefit over its intricate models (Belle & Papantonis, 2021). Automated validation systems in document processing environments validate extracted information by verifying publication details and standardized identifiers which leads to operational efficiency gains and lower need for human work. Document quality and extracted data inconsistencies both impact the accuracy of validation tests.
Furthermore, hybrid approaches which combine automated validation with human expertise provide better results for both reliability and accuracy. Research in information systems shows that expert input combined with structured system processes leads to better results because of increased quality and credibility of outputs (Hassmann & Westner, 2024).
The combination of automated data extraction with human verification processes should be integrated with rule-based systems to solve intricate assessment challenges. Therefore, combining rule-based validation with OCR and expert review, as implemented in FacultyLink, enhances both accuracy and reliability in faculty evaluation.
Document Processing and Verification Systems
Different fields use document processing systems which demonstrate operational principles that apply to other fields. The systems reach their verification accuracy through two processes which work together to verify results between automated testing and manual evaluation of special cases. Ines Mergel (2021) examined digital transformation in public administration and emphasized that digital systems must handle large volumes of documents efficiently by incorporating clear submission processes, exception handling, and a combination of automated validation and human review for complex cases. The document processing workflow at FacultyLink relies on these principles for its operational design.
Stefan Strohmeier (2020) examined document management and electronic human resource systems that validate records from diverse institutional sources. The systems use adaptable validation standards which create rules for document evaluation that permit different document types to be assessed while still needing to meet basic compliance standards. The approach works for FacultyLink because it manages publications and certificates and professional credentials from various institutions and publishers.  
Thanh Nam Nguyen et al. (2021) showed that OCR-based document processing systems achieve better operational performance because they automatically extract data while their output quality stays intact through their validation methods. This supports the integration of automated and rule-based validation systems such as FacultyLink. Most existing document processing systems fail to operate effectively because they need special adaptations, which remain absent for academic evaluation frameworks, including the DBM-CHED Joint Circular. Academic documents need systems that can implement structured scoring rules while maintaining compliance with established evaluation standards, which currently do not exist. The existing limitations show that FacultyLink needs to function as a dedicated solution because it meets specific operational needs.
Automated Scoring Systems
Automated point calculation systems resolve the computational challenges which arise when faculty evaluation follows DBM-CHED Joint Circular regulations. The 9th Cycle weighting system requires base points to be multiplied with KRA weights while specific activities have point limits which need to be distributed among co-authors according to Hernandez and Cruz (2021). The manual calculation process of these factors creates error and inconsistency possibilities which automated systems can eliminate. 
Poon, Lau, Yu, and Tang (2023) demonstrated that users create numerous mistakes with spreadsheet tools which use automated formulas because they depend on user behavior. Their research showed that data entry mistakes and computational parameter misuse and calculation logic misunderstandings lead to the most common inaccuracies. Users create unreliable results when they input values into wrong fields and use wrong multipliers and misunderstand evaluation rules. The research demonstrates how standalone spreadsheet systems have limitations which require integrated solutions for FacultyLink because it combines data entry with validation and automated computation in a single controlled workflow system.
Ramos and Santos (2021) reported that Philippine State Universities and Colleges (SUCs) have limited access to automated scoring systems, as most institutions still rely on manual evaluation conducted by committees. These manual processes, combined with complex scoring rules, often create bottlenecks in the promotion cycle and result in inconsistent scoring implementations.
Although automated tools exist, organizations continue to use manual spreadsheet calculations because they believe those methods provide better results. The existing system requires development because it needs a scoring system which centralizes assessment scores while maintaining proper evaluation standards. The FacultyLink system establishes an automated scoring system which complies with DBM–CHED Joint Circular requirements for faculty evaluation purposes.
Simulation and Decision Support Systems
Simulation tools which allow users to create multiple career paths through modeling have proven useful for professional development purposes. Research on digital career development systems demonstrates that simulation tools enable users to assess different work activities together with their corresponding decision-making methods. John L. Holland and Deirdre Hughes (2021) research showed that digital career planning tools assist users in tracking their professional development while determining which activities will help them reach their career objectives. Adrianna Kezar (2021) research on academic career development platforms shows that digital systems which enable users to create academic achievement projections will help instructors prepare for their promotion and tenure evaluations. The tools enable users to assess their present achievements which they need to fulfill institutional promotion requirements while determining their required accomplishments. The simulation features enable users to create their career paths while meeting promotional standards through active participation.
Simulation tools generate institutional analytics that serve as valuable resources for university administrators. Digital planning systems produce organizational data that support strategic decision-making, including trend analysis, workforce planning, and professional development evaluation (Mergel, 2021). These capabilities enable institutions to utilize aggregated simulation data to predict faculty promotion trends and develop more effective academic staffing strategies.
The current systems face a major restriction because they lack the ability to combine simulation capabilities with their real-time assessment functions. The existing systems require development of new systems which must integrate simulation capabilities and automatic scoring and validation systems. The FacultyLink system addresses this need by providing faculty members with rank advancement simulation capabilities which use actual assessment results to help them evaluate their progress and create effective professional development plans.

The systems are examined to determine their functional capabilities and their technological systems and their existing system constraints which researchers used as a foundation to identify the deficiencies which the FacultyLink system will resolve.
SUC Faculty Position Reclassification System (SFPRS)
The SUC Faculty Position Reclassification System (SFPRS) serves as an essential tool which State Universities and Colleges (SUCs) utilize to manage faculty promotion together with reclassification procedures. The system operates according to policies which the Commission on Higher Education and the Department of Budget and Management established through DBM–CHED Joint Circular No. 3, s. 2022 (DBM & CHED, 2022).
The system measures faculty performance through Key Result Areas (KRAs) which require faculty members to submit their academic records and research outputs and certifications as proof of their work. The evaluators will review each submission and apply the established criteria to determine the corresponding points.
The system provides a structured evaluation framework which organizations use to implement their assessments through manual or semi-digital methods that depend on spreadsheets and physical documents. The methods create delays while producing inconsistent scores and increasing the probability of computational mistakes. The lack of automated validation together with predictive tools prevents faculty members from understanding their evaluation readiness before they undergo formal assessment process.
Creatrix Campus Faculty Performance System
Several higher education institutions have implemented digital platforms to enhance their faculty evaluation process and administrative functions. The Creatrix Campus Faculty Performance System offers document management and performance tracking and report generation capabilities. The system improves institutional operations through better data accessibility and data organization and improved institutional operational processes.
The system uses web-based architecture and integrated database management to create a platform that supports institutional workflows. The system enables centralized data management while it allows users to track faculty activities during their teaching work and research work and administrative responsibilities. Research shows that most digital faculty management systems including Creatrix maintain their status as administrative tools because they lack complete intelligent evaluation features (Zawacki-Richter et al. 2023). 
The system has not yet achieved complete implementation of its advanced functions which include rule-based document validation and OCR verification and rank upgrade simulation. Faculty members at the school can track performance through system tools but they must interpret evaluation criteria manually which creates assessment problems because it generates extra work and decreases their evaluation results. The existing system needs FacultyLink because it provides intelligent validation and automated computation and simulation functions that meet DBM-CHED Joint Circular requirements.

Synthesis
The reviewed literature and related applications indicate that faculty evaluation systems under the DBM–CHED Joint Circular require complex procedures which need precise calculation and systematic verification and effective document handling. Existing studies demonstrate that digital transformation assists higher education institutions to achieve better administrative performance and improved transparency and enhanced data accessibility; however, currently available systems only provide basic administrative capabilities while lacking intelligent evaluation functionalities.
Technologies such as Optical Character Recognition (OCR) and rule-based validation systems and automated scoring mechanisms have demonstrated their capacity to improve document processing and ensure compliance and enhance performance evaluation accuracy within Key Result Area (KRA) frameworks. The analysis of related applications which include SFPRS and Creatrix Campus Faculty Performance System and Interfolio and SAP SuccessFactors shows that these systems mainly deal with three functions document management and performance tracking and workflow processes. The platforms provide incomplete advanced capabilities which include OCR-based document validation and automated scoring that follows DBM-CHED Joint Circular standards and tools for simulating rank upgrades.
The evaluation process becomes time-consuming because faculty members need to manually interpret evaluation criteria which creates inconsistencies and prevents transparent evaluation processes. The FacultyLink system developed in this study addresses identified gaps by unifying automated KRA-based scoring and OCR-enabled document validation and real-time compliance monitoring and rank upgrade simulation into a system that follows the DBM-CHED Joint Circular framework.

METHODOLOGY
This chapter presents the methodology used in the development and evaluation of the FacultyLink system. It discusses the research design, data gathering procedures, and analysis techniques used in the study. It also describes the current faculty evaluation process and outlines the system architecture, development process, conceptual design, and implementation. Research Methodology
The study applied the Descriptive-Developmental Research Methodology in the design, development, and evaluation of the FacultyLink system. The descriptive aspect of the study was used to examine the existing faculty evaluation process under the DBM-CHED Joint Circular. Through this approach, the researchers identified several issues in the current process, including manual computation of Key Result Area (KRA) points, difficulties in document management, and the lack of transparency during evaluation procedures. 
The developmental aspect of the study focused on the creation of the FacultyLink system as a solution to the identified problems. The system was developed using an adapted Agile Software Development Methodology inspired by the Agile Manifesto introduced by Kent Beck and other software developers in 2001. Agile emphasizes iterative development, collaboration, flexibility, and continuous improvement throughout the software development process.
For this study, the researchers adapted the Agile methodology into seven phases: Planning, Design, Development, Testing, Implementation, Launch, and Maintenance. The methodology enabled the researchers to continuously improve the system based on user feedback and system requirements. Through this approach, important system features such as automated scoring, document validation, and rank upgrade simulation were effectively developed and refined.
The use of this methodology provided a systematic process for evaluating the existing faculty assessment procedures while ensuring that the developed system addressed user needs and complied with DBM-CHED Joint Circular requirements..

Planning
The research team began their work by collecting system requirements which they arranged into established structural components. The researchers gathered data by conducting interviews with faculty members, evaluators, and administrators, and through document analysis of the DBM–CHED Joint Circular guidelines. The stage aimed to achieve three goals, which included understanding how faculty evaluation operated, identifying its existing problems, and establishing system needs. The system design and development process started from this stage.
Design
The design phase of the project included the development of the system architecture, which consisted of database design, system workflow creation, and user interface development for both web and mobile applications. The system operated through a structured framework that enabled users to upload documents, validate them, and execute scoring and simulation tasks. The design team created an interface that faculty members and reviewers could easily use because it combined user-friendly elements with intuitive navigation.
Development
The development process will transform the system design into a functional application. The team will develop essential system features which will include document upload and management, OCR text extraction, automated KRA scoring, real-time dashboard visualization, and rank upgrade simulation. The team will also add reviewer validation features which will enable authorized users to evaluate submitted documents and provide feedback.
Testing
The system will reach its testing milestone during this phase, where its performance, accuracy, and reliability will be assessed. The testing process will utilize multiple test cases to discover system errors, inconsistencies, and performance limitations. The team will implement essential modifications and system enhancements to achieve compliance with required standards and to guarantee correct system operation across various operational scenarios.
Implementation (Launch/Deployment)
The system will be presented to users for real-world testing during its deployment phase. Faculty members and reviewers will be able to interact with the system and perform tasks such as document submission, validation, and self-assessment. The feedback collected from this stage will be used to improve the system.
Support and Maintenance

The maintenance of the FacultyLink system during this phase will ensure its ongoing functionality, relevance, and up-to-date status throughout its operational life.
Data Gathering Procedure
The data gathering process was conducted to collect relevant information necessary for the design and development of the FacultyLink system. The researchers used interviews and document analysis and system observation to study the existing faculty evaluation system which operates under the DBM–CHED Joint Circular. The methods enabled identification of system requirements and existing challenges and user needs.
 Research Instrument
The study utilized a semi-structured interview guide as the primary research instrument. The interview questions were classified into categories such as current faculty evaluation procedures, challenges in document submission and KRA computation, document validation and compliance monitoring, and recommended system features. The semi-structured interview was conducted with different classifications of respondents, namely faculty members, reviewers/evaluators, and administrators or client representatives. The format allowed the researchers to ask follow-up questions to obtain more detailed responses relevant to the study.
 Sampling Method
The study used purposive sampling to choose its study participants. The researchers selected respondents from a particular group who had direct experience with the faculty evaluation process defined by the DBM–CHED Joint Circular. The group included a client, a system reviewer, and a faculty member with practical evaluation process knowledge. The experts collected data which matched study requirements while ensuring that data remained reliable and applicable to study goals.
 Statistical Treatment of Data
The researchers used descriptive analysis to interpret the data gathered from the interviews. Responses were organized and analyzed according to common themes and patterns relevant to the study objectives.
To evaluate the usability of the FacultyLink system, the researchers will utilize the System Usability Scale (SUS) developed by John Brooke (1996). The SUS is a standardized questionnaire consisting of ten statements measured using a five-point Likert scale ranging from Strongly Disagree (1) to Strongly Agree (5). The responses will be computed using the standard SUS formula:
SUS Score=(∑Adjusted Scores)×2.5
The computed SUS scores will be interpreted to determine the overall usability, acceptability, and user satisfaction of the system.
Description of the Current System (Information Gathering)
The current faculty rank advancement system which operates under the DBM-CHED Joint Circular functions through manual methods and partial digital processes instead of using a centralized system to manage its operations. Faculty members need to submit different documents which include their teaching records and research publications and advisership certifications and other supporting materials according to data from the selected interview respondents. The documents evaluate performance through four Key Result Areas (KRAs) which include Instruction, Research and Innovation, Extension or Community Services, and Professional Development.
The interview results show that the submission and review process operates through email communication. Faculty members compile their documentary requirements in digital format and send them to the assigned reviewer or evaluation committee via email. The reviewers access the submitted documents through their email accounts which contain all submitted files that need to be evaluated. The document organization process requires fundamental digital tools which include email and spreadsheets and word processing applications that create challenges for efficient file management and tracking and organization.
The KRA point computation process uses manual methods and spreadsheet tools for execution which needs precise data input and proper scoring rule implementation. The procedure requires extensive time to complete because it involves multiple stages which are susceptible to mistakes that arise when researchers work together on projects. The evaluation happens exclusively at designated assessment times because the system lacks automated document verification capabilities before submission.
As a result, faculty members face difficulties with compliance requirements which results in incomplete submissions and evaluation process delays because their submissions contain inaccurate information. The existing system does not include a self-assessment tool which enables faculty members to track their progress and monitor their KRA scores and assess their rank advancement eligibility.
The identified problems demonstrate the requirement for FacultyLink because it functions as a self-assessment platform which allows faculty to handle their documents and assess their performance while creating rank eligibility simulations. The system aims to reduce computation errors in the evaluation process.

Automated KRA Scoring Algorithm
      The FacultyLink system utilizes a rule-based decision algorithm to compute faculty evaluation processes uploaded documents through  structured stages, including data extraction, classification, validation, scoring, and rank simulation. Upon document upload, the system applies Tesseract OCR to extract relevant information such as title, author(s), and content. The extracted data are then analyzed using predefined keyword-based rules to classify the document into one of the Key Result Areas (KRAs):  Instruction, Research and Innovation, Extension, or Professional Development. In cases where multiple conditions are satisfied, a priority based rule is applied, and ambiguous results are forwarded to the reviewer.
       The system then performs validation by checking required fields and detecting duplicates using file hashing. Documents with missing fields or low-confidence extraction is flagged for review. Once validated, the system computes the score using the formula:
 Final Score = Base Points × Contribution Percentage 
 Where the contribution percentage is based on the number of authors. For example, a 20-point publication with two authors results in 10 points per author.
      Pseudocode of the Automated KRA Scoring Algorithm
BEGIN
INPUT: Uploaded Document D
1. Extract text using OCR
2. Identify document type based on keywords
3. Validate required fields (title, author, date)
   IF invalid → send to reviewer
4. Assign base points based on KRA
5. Apply contribution percentage (if co-authored)
6. Compute final score
7. Check duplicate
   IF duplicate → ignore
8. Store and aggregate scores
9. Perform rank simulation
OUTPUT: KRA Score and Rank Result
END

 Scope and Limitation of the Study
The FacultyLink system functions as a centralized web and mobile-based self-assessment platform designed to assist faculty members in preparing for rank upgrade and reclassification in accordance with the DBM–CHED Joint Circular. The system enables users to upload and manage documentary evidence and utilizes Tesseract OCR for document processing, rule-based validation for compliance checking, and automated computation of Key Result Area (KRA) scores. It also provides a rank upgrade simulation feature to estimate eligibility and a performance monitoring dashboard with a notification system that allows reviewers to provide feedback on document status, missing requirements, and compliance verification. 
However, the system has several limitations. The official evaluation by authorized DBM–CHED committees will not be replaced by this tool which serves only as a preparatory tool. The accuracy of OCR and validation processes depends on the quality and clarity of the uploaded documents. The system operates according to current DBM–CHED Joint Circular guidelines and needs policy updates when there are changes. The system cannot be accessed offline and requires a stable internet connection. The system does not verify the authenticity of documents and relies on reviewer validation for final approval.
